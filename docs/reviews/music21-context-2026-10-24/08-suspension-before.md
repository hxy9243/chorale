## The event across measures 4–5

The soprano C is **tied across the barline**. Be careful here: this is *not* a true dissonant suspension. The C is the 5th of F major in m4 and remains a **chord tone** (the 3rd) of A minor in m5. So the tie functions as a **common tone** binding the F→Am change, not a suspension requiring resolution. The genuinely dissonant note is the **B on beat 2 of m5**: against A–C–E it forms a 9th above the bass A and is best heard as a **non-chord tone** (passing/escape character) connecting the tied C to the D of m6. Calling it a "4–3 suspension" would be a mislabel, since the tied note is consonant in both sonorities.

## Harmony and inversion, measures 5–7

- **m5:** A minor, **root position** (bass A) — functions as ii in C major
- **m6:** C major, **second inversion** (bass G) — a 6/4 chord; given its placement between Am and G7, it behaves as a **passing/pedal-type 6/4**, not an independent structural chord. The soprano D is a 9th (non-chord tone) that should resolve, ideally down to C
- **m7:** G7, **root position** (bass G) — the dominant seventh
- **m8:** resolves to C major, confirming the tonal cadence

So the functional progression is essentially **ii–(passing 6/4)–V7–I**.

## Revision suggestion

Don't relabel the harmony on every beat. Hear mm. 4–8 as one extended motion: **F–Am–C/G–G7–C**, a dominant-preparation → dominant → tonic arc. Concretely: check the soprano D in m6 — as a dissonant 9th over the 6/4, it wants to resolve downward to C, and the B in m5 should be understood as a melodic connector, not evidence of a new chord. Ask "what is this beat *prolonging* or *passing through*?" before assigning a fresh Roman numeral.
