## Answers to your specific questions

**Applied dominant:** m5, E major (V/vi), resolving to m6, A minor (vi). The G♯ resolves up to A in m6 — a textbook applied dominant. (m11's G7 → m12 C is a regular dominant, not an applied one.)

**Inversions:** m3 is genuinely a first-inversion C major (bass E). **m7 is not an inversion** — the bass G is the root of the G–D–A sonority. With no third present, it is best described as an open fifth G–D with an added A (possibly a suspension or incomplete dominant); any Roman-numeral label here is tentative.

**m11 sonority:** G–B–F, a root-position dominant seventh on G, resolving conventionally to C in m12.

**Does the m5 G♯ affect m13?** No. The G♯ is a local chromaticism that resolves to A in m6 and is immediately cancelled; m13 contains F–F–A over F with no G at all, and the surrounding music is fully diatonic to C major.
