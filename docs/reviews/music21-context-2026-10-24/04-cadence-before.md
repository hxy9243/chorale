Progression: **I – IV – V – I** in G major, all root-position triads, no sevenths or passing tones — a clean, textbook harmonic skeleton.

## The final cadence

The strict soprano-and-bass condition for a **perfect authentic cadence** requires:
1. V moving to I (satisfied: m.3 D major → m.4 G major),
2. Bass ending on scale degree 1 (satisfied: bass G),
3. **Soprano ending on scale degree 1** (satisfied: soprano ends on G, the tonic).

So yes — the cadence **meets the strict condition and is a perfect authentic cadence**. Both outer voices land on the tonic G, and the dominant is in root position. (If the soprano had ended on B or D instead, it would only be an imperfect authentic cadence.)

## Aesthetic notes (separate from the analysis above)

- The voice leading is smooth: soprano B–C–A–G and alto G–G–F♯–D move mostly by step or common tone; the alto's F♯ resolves down to D, and the leading-tone F♯ is in an inner voice, so the "leading tone must ascend" rule is relaxed there.
- One mild point: the tenor leaps D→E→D→B; the D→B leap into the final chord is fine but slightly angular.
- Harmonically it is very plain (all root-position, no inversions, no seventh chords) — perfectly idiomatic for a cadence exercise, though rhythmically static since every chord lasts a full measure.
