### Notational facts
Pitches below use middle C = C4.

- **Voice order:** U remains above L throughout; there is no voice crossing or overlap.
- **Perfect intervals:** There are no consecutive parallel fifths or octaves between successive note-onset positions. For example:
  - **m.2, beats 1→3:** U moves D4→G4 while L moves B2→G2, arriving at a double octave by **contrary motion**.
  - **m.3 beat 4→m.4 beat 1:** U moves B3→C4 while L moves G3→C3, arriving at an octave by **contrary motion**.
- **Dissonances:** At **m.1 beat 2** and **m.3 beat 2**, U’s F4 sounds against L’s sustained C3: a perfect eleventh, conventionally dissonant in two-part counterpoint.
  - **m.1:** E4–F4–G4 makes F4 a stepwise passing tone.
  - **m.3:** E4–F4–D4 approaches F4 by ascending step and leaves by descending third: an escape-tone contour, not ordinary passing motion.

### Style-dependent concerns
- In strict species-style counterpoint, **m.3 beat 2, U F4**, is the clearest concern: the dissonance is left by leap rather than step. A freer tonal style may accept it as a weak-beat escape tone.
- **m.1 beat 2, U F4**, is normally acceptable as a weak-beat passing dissonance, although the precise rules depend on the counterpoint system.
- **L, m.2 beats 1→3, B2→G2:** the scale-degree-seven note does not rise to C. That is not automatically an error; its acceptability depends on its harmonic and contrapuntal role.
- L’s **G2→C3** across m.2→3 and **C3→G3→C3** across m.3→4 are conspicuous leaps, but not intrinsically prohibited.

### Limitations
No species, harmonic analysis, or stylistic rule set is supplied. Thus the interval and motion facts are determinate, but categorical judgments about dissonance treatment, bass leaps, and cadence quality are not.
